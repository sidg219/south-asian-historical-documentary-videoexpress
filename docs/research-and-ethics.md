# Research and representation

## Claim labels

- **Documented:** directly supported by a cited inscription, text, object, archaeological report, contemporary account or reputable scholarly source.
- **Interpreted:** a reasoned scholarly interpretation where the evidence admits more than one reading.
- **Reconstructed:** an audiovisual choice used to help the audience imagine a setting or process; it is not evidence by itself.

Keep a claim ledger with the exact narration sentence, source, date accessed, confidence and label. If sources disagree, say so briefly in narration or on screen. Use “may have”, “likely” and “the evidence suggests” where appropriate.

## Representation rules

Show social variety: workers, traders, artisans, pilgrims, farmers, officials, domestic spaces and children only when age-appropriate and historically supported. Avoid treating modern national, religious, caste or regional identities as unchanged categories in the past. Do not collapse “India” into a single culture; name the region, polity, language community or network.

Never fabricate a quotation, translation, inscription, manuscript page or eyewitness scene. Do not use a modern photograph with altered colours as if it were historical footage. Add “visual reconstruction” to reconstructed sequences and preserve source credits in the final description.
